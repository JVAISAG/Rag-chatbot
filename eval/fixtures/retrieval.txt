Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) supplies retrieved evidence to a language model before it answers a question. It can use a changing document collection without retraining the language model.

Dense retrieval encodes questions and passages as vectors. Sparse retrieval matches words. BM25 is a sparse ranking function that considers term frequency, inverse document frequency, and document length.

Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF) combines ordered result lists using ranks rather than directly comparing their incompatible raw scores. A cross-encoder jointly scores a question and a candidate passage after initial retrieval.
